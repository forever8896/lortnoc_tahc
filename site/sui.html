Sui · Walrus · Seal

Every message encrypted, anonymized, and stored immutably.

On a network that can’t read what it’s holding, behind a rule anyone can audit.

Why we built on it

If we hold your messages, we can be made to hand them over.

The threat model isn’t only Chat Control — it’s the next order served on whoever runs the service. So the constraint was absolute: never hold plaintext, never hold a key. That rules out our own infrastructure, and it rules out cloud storage with encryption we administer, because “we promise not to look” is a policy and policies change under subpoena.

What’s left needs two things that almost nothing offers together: storage that can’t read what it stores, and an access rule nobody can quietly edit. Walrus and Seal are the only pair we found where both are structural rather than promised.

How it works here

  1. Batch. Messages group into one blob with Quilt — up to 420× cheaper than storing them one by one.
  2. Encrypt. Seal threshold-encrypts the batch. No single party holds the key.
  3. Scatter. Walrus erasure-codes it across the network. Every node holds a fragment it can’t read.
  4. Point. A ConversationHead Sui object tracks the current blob. Blobs never change; only the pointer moves.
  5. Find. An ENS text record points at the manifest, so any device with the user’s secret rebuilds their inbox.
  6. Open. Decrypted client-side with id_seal, derived on-device. Reading your own history is always free.

For the Walrus and Seal team

Three decisions you made are the reason this project has the shape it does.

Quilt

Most blob stores treat small objects as somebody else’s problem. Chat is nothing but small objects — a message is a few hundred bytes, and per-blob overhead would have made per-message storage economically absurd. Quilt is the difference between a demo and something we could actually afford to run.

seal_approve as arbitrary Move

The obvious way to build access control for threshold encryption is a fixed vocabulary: allowlist, time-lock, token-gate. You made the policy a Move function evaluated by dry-run instead, so it can be anything expressible on Sui.

That’s what makes a nullifier-gated policy conceivable at all. We’re on the session-key path today, but we designed toward the harder version precisely because you left the ceiling that high.

Immutable blobs, mutable pointer

Splitting a durable append-only log from a Sui object that moves is a separation we’d otherwise have had to invent badly. Our ConversationHead is your primitive with two extra fields.

And RedStuff’s self-healing is what lets us tell users their data outlives us without hedging the sentence.
