Sui · Walrus · Seal

Every message encrypted, anonymized, and stored immutably.

On a network that can’t read what it’s holding, behind a rule anyone can audit.

Why we built on it

Lortnoc Tahc hides real messages inside platforms that scan everything. Those conversations still have to live somewhere — and if that somewhere is ours, we’ve just made ourselves the easiest thing in the system to subpoena.

So we needed two properties, together: storage that can’t read what it holds, and an access rule we can’t quietly change.

Walrus gives the first — erasure-coded fragments spread across nodes, none of which ever holds a whole blob. Seal gives the second — decryption gated by seal_approve, a Move function running on Sui, rather than a conditional inside our backend.

Neither alone would have been enough. We needed the pair.

How it works here

  1. Encrypt. Seal encrypts each message to an identity namespaced under its conversation object. No single party holds the key.
  2. Scatter. Walrus erasure-codes it across the network. Every node holds a fragment it can’t read.
  3. Point. A ConversationHead Sui object tracks the current blob. Blobs never change; only the pointer moves.
  4. Find. Conversations announce themselves as ConversationCreated events on Sui, so any device holding the user’s secret rebuilds their inbox from chain with nothing stored by us.
  5. Open. Your Seal session key is signed by a Sui keypair derived on-device from your master secret — so the key servers answer to you, not to us. Reading your own history is always free.

For the Walrus and Seal team

Three decisions you made are the reason this project has the shape it does.

Quilt

Most blob stores treat small objects as somebody else’s problem. Chat is nothing but small objects — a message is a few hundred bytes, and Walrus bills an encoded floor per blob regardless of size, so one-blob-per-message is a pricing catastrophe at scale. Quilt is the answer to that, and at 660 items per blob the saving is roughly 650×.

We are not using it yet, and the measurement is why. We wrote a three-message quilt to testnet: it came back 445,556 bytes. Quilt pads to its sliver structure, so a small quilt has a sizeable floor of its own — and a 1:1 conversation produces messages one at a time, never filling one. Batching would mean holding a message back from its recipient to save storage we are not yet spending. So we ship one blob per message today, with the switch costed and the read path already proven against per-patch aggregator reads.

seal_approve as arbitrary Move

The obvious way to build access control for threshold encryption is a fixed vocabulary: allowlist, time-lock, token-gate. You made the policy a Move function evaluated by dry-run instead, so it can be anything expressible on Sui.

That’s what makes a nullifier-gated policy conceivable at all. We’re on the session-key path today, but we designed toward the harder version precisely because you left the ceiling that high.

Immutable blobs, mutable pointer

Splitting a durable append-only log from a Sui object that moves is a separation we’d otherwise have had to invent badly. Our ConversationHead is your primitive with two extra fields.

And RedStuff’s self-healing is what lets us tell users their data outlives us without hedging the sentence.
