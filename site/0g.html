0G · Confidential Compute

The judgement that makes cover text pass.

Every message is written several different ways, and sealed compute picks the one that reads most like a human being sent it. What it judges cannot be logged, retained, or read.

Why it’s essential

Steganography fails the moment the disguise reads like a disguise.

Hiding a message inside ordinary language is only half the problem, and it is the easy half. What gets people caught is fluency. Cover text that is grammatical but subtly wrong — an odd turn of phrase, a sentence no real person would send to a friend — is itself a signal. Automated scanning is extremely good at noticing text that does not sit right, and a message flagged as anomalous is worse than one sent in the clear, because it marks you as someone with something to hide.

So something has to score naturalness. And that scorer necessarily sees candidate text derived from your message. Running it against a normal inference endpoint would mean handing that text to a provider who logs prompts — manufacturing exactly the record this product exists to prevent.

Confidential compute is what makes the judgement possible without creating the evidence. The scoring happens somewhere that cannot keep what it scored.

Why the encoder is local and the judge is not

This is the part most descriptions of “AI-powered encryption” get wrong, so it is worth being exact.

Reversible steganography has an unforgiving requirement: the encoder and the decoder must walk byte-identical probability distributions, or the message does not come back. Not “comes back garbled” — does not come back at all.

Hosted GPU inference cannot promise that. Shared batched fleets, no seed guarantee; even at temperature zero, two runs of the same request can diverge. Route the reversible half of a codec through it and you have built something that silently loses messages.

So we split the work by which half tolerates nondeterminism. The reversible arithmetic coding runs on a pinned local model, deterministic and byte-exact. The naturalness judgement — a preference between finished candidates, not a computation anyone has to reproduce — runs on 0G.

That is not a workaround for a limitation. It is the correct allocation of the two halves: determinism where reversibility depends on it, sealed compute where privacy does.

How it works here

  1. Encrypt locally. The extension encrypts your message with AES-SIV under a key derived on your device. Everything downstream sees ciphertext and nothing else — never plaintext, never a key.
  2. Generate several candidates. A pinned local GPT-2 arithmetic-codes that ciphertext into multiple different cover texts. Same payload, genuinely different phrasings.
  3. Score inside sealed compute. The candidates go to our 0G sidecar, where confidential inference rates which one reads most naturally — which sounds most like a message a person would actually send.
  4. Send the winner. The highest-scoring cover text goes into the compose box. That sentence about coffee is the entire record the platform keeps.
  5. Decode. Your correspondent’s local codec reverses the arithmetic coding and verifies the AES-SIV tag. Because both ends run the same pinned model deterministically, it round-trips exactly. The auth tag doubles as the detector: a valid tag means it was one of ours, an invalid one means it really was just small talk.

What 0G actually sees

Candidate cover texts. Those encode ciphertext — never plaintext — and are useless without a key that never leaves your device. If the sidecar were fully compromised tomorrow, the attacker would hold a pile of innocuous sentences about coffee and weather, and no way to open any of them.

The other half: anonymous membership

0G Chain also hosts the membership contract, which solves a problem that has nothing to do with language and everything to do with money.

Paying inserts a commitment into an on-chain Merkle set. Later, claiming a handle proves you know the secret behind some commitment in that set and emits a nullifier that can only be spent once. Observers see that a wallet paid, and that the set grew. They never see which handle that payment unlocked.

The chain of wallet → commitment is public. The chain of commitment → nullifier → handle is not. That link is severed by construction, not by our discretion.

What we don’t claim

0G does not run the encoder, and it cannot. Any claim that our steganographic model runs on confidential compute would be false. Hosted inference cannot provide the byte-determinism reversibility requires. We investigated deploying our own model into their trusted environment, which would genuinely solve it — the 0G team confirmed that isn’t supported today. So we say what is true instead.

Sealed compute sees candidate cover text. That is ciphertext-equivalent rather than plaintext, but it is not nothing, and we will not describe it as zero exposure.

Best-of-N improves how natural the text reads. It is not a guarantee. A determined, purpose-built classifier is a different adversary from a general scanning pipeline, and we don’t claim immunity from one by citing performance against the other.
