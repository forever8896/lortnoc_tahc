A space is an ENS name. Its rules are its records.

When you buy an NFT Space in Everywhere, you get a real ENS v2 name — yourclub.space.lortnoctahc.eth — with its own resolver, owned by your wallet. Which NFT collection opens its posts, and who is banned, live in that name's text records. The gate reads them from ENS on every unlock. We hold no admin key over your space.

What the owner sees

Extension ⚙ → Spaces → pick a name → pick a collection (contract + chain, checked live) → Buy. From then on, anyone posting can choose "NFT holders of yourclub.space…" or "Verified humans of yourclub.space…", and only readers who qualify right now can open it. Change the collection or ban someone by editing your ENS records — in the extension, or in any ENS tool.

Step by step — from purchase to an unlocked post

Pay on Ethereum

LortnocSpaces.buySpace(label, owner) — 0.005 ETH (the first 10 get 90% off, enforced in the contract via currentPrice()). It emits SpacePurchased; the funds go straight to the treasury.

The relayer issues the name

POST /space reads that purchase from chain and calls SpaceRegistrar, which in one transaction deploys the space's own PermissionedResolver proxy from ENS's canonical VerifiableFactory, writes addr + eth.lortnoc.space.token, grants every role to the owner, revokes its own, and registers the label in our SpaceRegistry.

A real v2 registry tree

RootRegistry → eth (ETHRegistry) → lortnoctahc (LortnocRegistry) → space (SpaceRegistry) → your label. Each hop is its own contract — v2 hierarchical registries. Standard ENS tools resolve it through the canonical UniversalResolver.

The rules are records

eth.lortnoc.space.token = a CAIP-19 collection, e.g. eip155:11155111/erc721:0xc854…43f0. eth.lortnoc.space.bans = banned member pseudonyms. Owners can delegate just the bans record to a moderator — v2 per-record roles (Enhanced Access Control).

The gate reads ENS live, and fails closed

On every unlock, the gate resolves the space through the UniversalResolver, confirms it exists with UniversalHelper.findExactOwner (in v2 an unclaimed label resolves to empty records — "resolves" ≠ "exists"), then checks the reader's wallet holds the collection. If a read fails on every RPC, the post stays shut.

What the proof shows

Canonical resolutionviem's default UniversalResolver is ENS's own proxy, walking ENS's RootRegistry
Every hop is a registryeach label has its own subregistry; the leaf knows its parent (getParent)
Ownership, ENS's wayfindExactOwner = the registry's owner = the addr record
Its own resolverfactory-verified PermissionedResolverImpl, not a shared resolver
Per-record rolesowner holds SET_TEXT; our registrar holds no role; a stranger's write reverts
Fails closedthe gate's ENS reads use fallback RPCs; if all fail, nothing unlocks

Honest limits. ENS v2 lives on Sepolia today, and ENS resets Sepolia every month or so — names don't carry over, so we re-migrate (preflight.mjs tells us when). The purchase contract is also live on Ethereum mainnet; issuing mainnet names waits for ENS v2 on mainnet. Holder checks read chain state at unlock time, so selling the NFT closes future posts but can't un-read one already opened.
