Business model

We sell Lortnoctahc DM accounts. The costs are small, and they scale in flat steps.

One-time payment per account, settled on 0G in a way that can’t be traced back to the handle it bought. Infrastructure that grows $30 at a time.

Revenue

A Lortnoctahc DM account is an ENS sub-name. To get one, you buy it. That’s the business — not ads, not data, not a subscription nobody wants to think about.

$50 USDC, one time. The first 100 accounts go for $1, because early users are worth more to us as proof than as revenue.

The payment rail is itself a feature

Payments settle on 0G mainnet and are gated by zero-knowledge nullifiers. Paying inserts a commitment; claiming a handle proves you know the secret behind some commitment and burns a single-use nullifier.

The result: we can verify that an account was paid for without ever learning which wallet paid for which handle. Most privacy products have to ask you to trust their billing system. Ours structurally can’t link the two — which means we can sell to exactly the people who would never hand a card to a company like ours.

Cost of goods sold

Encrypted messaging over Telegram runs on a codec service we host. One capacity unit costs $30/day and serves roughly 800 daily active users, on an unlimited-use model — heavy simultaneous load makes it slower, not more expensive.

When demand outgrows a unit, we buy another one. Capacity scales in flat $30/day increments with no step-function capex, so COGS tracks usage closely in both directions.

Where a payment goes

Each $50 funds the account it bought: minting the user’s sub-name, and standing up a Walrus wallet on Sui that we fund by delegation so storage is sponsored rather than billed to the user. Nobody has to hold a token or think about gas to use a messaging app.

At current scale those on-chain costs are immaterial next to the $30/day compute line.

Unit economics

A full capacity unit at 800 paying accounts is $40,000 of one-time revenue against $10,950 of annual cost — a 73% gross margin in year one.

The honest tension is that revenue is one-time and cost is recurring, so the model depends on what share of accounts are actually daily active. At full daily engagement, $50 buys 3.7 years of service. Real messaging products don’t look like that.

At the engagement ratios consumer messaging actually sees, a one-time $50 covers well over a decade of service per account. If it turns out we’re wrong about that, the fix is a renewal on the sub-name — which ENS already models natively, and which we’d rather introduce from data than assume from a spreadsheet.

Where the model extends

Short and premium handles price above $50 on scarcity alone. Sub-names under an organisation’s own name are the obvious business tier. Neither is built yet, and neither is needed for the core model to work.

What we don’t know yet

Sub-names are minted on Sepolia and storage runs on Sui testnet today. Mainnet costs for minting and for sponsored Walrus storage are not yet measured. We expect them to stay well under the compute line and to remain sponsored rather than passed to users, but we haven’t paid them yet and won’t pretend otherwise.

The $30/day figure is real and being paid now.
